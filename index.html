About Erin

My path to this work began with my own fear of death.

Like many people, I spent much of my life afraid of the process of dying. When my grandmother became ill in 2025, I wanted to understand what lay ahead—not only so I would be less afraid, but so I could be truly present with her and make the time we had together meaningful.

I began seeking out everything I could find. I learned from hospice educators, watched and listened to people speak openly about the dying process, and sought resources that helped me understand what happens at the end of life.

Somewhere in that process, something changed. The subject I had once feared became something I felt deeply called to understand and, ultimately, to help others navigate.

That calling led me to end-of-life doula work and to Light at the Threshold.

I believe support around death should not begin only in someone's final hours. You may be thinking about your own mortality and wanting to plan ahead. You may have received a life-limiting diagnosis. You may be caring for someone you love and wondering what comes next. You may need someone to sit vigil when death is near. Or a death may have already occurred, and you are trying to find your footing in the life that follows.

Wherever you are in that process, you don't have to arrive knowing exactly what you need. We can begin there together.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

What is an end-of-life doula?

An end-of-life doula provides non-medical emotional, practical, and informational support around death and dying. Support can begin well before the final days of life.

Is an end-of-life doula the same as hospice?

No. Hospice provides medical and interdisciplinary end-of-life care. An end-of-life doula has a separate, non-medical role and can complement hospice and other professional care.

Do I need to be actively dying to contact you?

No. You may reach out while thinking about future end-of-life wishes, after receiving a serious diagnosis, while caring for someone else, during the dying process, or following a death.

Do I need to have a particular religious belief?

No. Support is centered on you and your values. People of all faiths, spiritual traditions, philosophies, and those who do not identify with a religious or spiritual tradition are welcome.

What are legacy services?

Legacy work offers a way to preserve memories, stories, experiences, and a person's voice for the people they love. Projects can include recorded interviews, edited recordings, and written narratives created from recorded conversations.
